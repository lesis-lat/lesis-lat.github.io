Your security R&D partner

The future of the internet is still unwritten. We are here to make sure the next chapter is written securely.

Our approach is grounded in a thorough attacker's perspective and in research-driven thinking. We understand what each organization needs and adapt to requirements that differ from one to the next, so that the investment in security becomes a differentiator for the business, and the investments already made are preserved. LESIS works with fintechs, retail, healthcare, telecommunications and lawtechs.

As an independent institution, with no ties to other companies or investors, we are neutral when we evaluate products and technology, and our work is judged on its quality alone. Our consultants bring more than ten years of experience and have reported vulnerabilities to D-Link (CVE-2020-9376 and CVE-2020-9377), Apple, Activision, Oracle and MISP (CVE-2020-28043).

How we execute

Security is not linear. Neither is our execution.

In the traditional model, a single analyst conducts the whole assessment. LESIS runs each project as a collaborative effort, with several specialists working on the same system at the same time, each bringing a different background to it.

  1. Greater technical coverage

    More components examined, and more than one way of examining each of them.

  2. Fewer individual biases

    No single assumption about the system goes unchallenged.

  3. Cross-validation of findings

    Each vulnerability is confirmed by more than one engineer before it is recorded.

  4. Deeper scenarios

    Time to follow a chain of weaknesses to what it actually exposes.

What we do

We follow security problems from design to code to execution.

Application security, architecture review and software supply chain security, supported by static analysis, fuzzing, reverse engineering and exploit development. We bring these disciplines together to investigate your system.

  1. Review the system's design

    Map trust boundaries, review architecture and assess security controls to understand how design decisions affect the system's exposure to attacks.

  2. Investigate how it can fail

    Combine pentesting, code review, static analysis and reverse engineering to find weaknesses, then develop exploits to verify how they can be used and establish their impact.

  3. Build tools to look deeper

    Build fuzzing harnesses, custom analyzers and instrumentation to reach complex code paths, examine runtime behavior and investigate weaknesses that need a closer look.

How an engagement runs

Understand the system. Leave your team stronger.

We work with your engineers to turn a security question into a result they can use: a design, a technical decision, a research finding or a working tool. The methods and deliverables follow the problem. Throughout the engagement, we build the understanding your team needs to carry the work forward.

  1. 01 Context

    Understand the context

    Study the systems, workflows and requirements the work depends on. We examine existing research and available approaches, identify assumptions and establish which questions need further investigation.

  2. 02 Scope

    Define the outcome together

    Start with what your team needs to understand, decide or build. We agree on the objectives, constraints and deliverables, whether the work concerns an architecture, a security control, a technical investigation or a new tool.

  3. 03 Approach

    Choose an approach we can evaluate

    Work through the technical options and tradeoffs with your engineers. Define the methods, interfaces and success criteria so we can assess the result against the problem it needs to solve.

  4. 04 Development

    Develop the solution through evidence

    Research, experiment and build in iterations, adjusting the approach as the evidence develops. When the scope includes custom harnesses, analyzers or instrumentation, we build them for your engineers to keep using, with instructions for running them and interpreting the results.

  5. 05 Validation

    Test it against real requirements

    Evaluate the result against the agreed criteria and real operating conditions. Document its behavior, limitations and tradeoffs, and resolve gaps with your engineers. Where applicable, deliver reproducible test cases and regression checks so your team can verify fixes and detect recurring weaknesses.

  6. 06 Handover

    Equip your team to carry it forward

    Deliver the agreed artifacts and explain the reasoning through technical walkthroughs. Depending on the scope, these include threat models, root-cause analysis and documented design decisions your engineers can apply as the system evolves. Suitable results can also become public research or open-source contributions, when agreed.

Security Engineering

Work alongside our engineers on architecture, security controls and the technical questions that arise as your product evolves.

We review designs, map trust boundaries and data flows, and help your team assess security decisions before and during implementation. The collaboration can cover a new system, a migration or changes to an existing product, with focused investigation and validation where needed.

Vulnerability Research

Investigate how your system can be compromised, with reproducible evidence and guidance for remediation.

We scope the assessment around the product's threat model and attack surface. Depending on the system, the work combines application and infrastructure testing, architecture and code review, and reverse engineering. We investigate attack chains, explain their impact and retest the fixes.

Custom Security Tooling

Build the fuzzers, analyzers and instrumentation needed to investigate your system.

We develop tools around specific formats, interfaces and behaviors, from test harnesses to custom analysis. The scope defines what the tool must examine and how your team will use it, whether for a focused investigation or repeated testing.

Incident Investigations

We do not run the response; we join the team that does, for one part of it: establishing how the intrusion began, and proving it rather than asserting it.

Most of what an incident demands belongs to the team already handling it: containment, eradication, communication. We do not take that over. We join for the way in. We take the evidence back to the specific weakness that made the intrusion possible and then demonstrate that weakness, reproducing the path instead of naming a probable cause, so the conclusion still holds when someone questions it. What comes out is not a narrative but a finding: an entry point with evidence, a severity, a deadline and a retest, linked on the platform to the affected systems and to the timeline of the investigation it came from. This is worth commissioning for a narrow set of incidents. When the way in is already understood, or when nothing technical was exploited at all, there is nothing for us to prove, and we say so before the work starts rather than after.

Due Diligence

We assess what a transaction brings with it, a company, a codebase or a product you are about to adopt, on the timeline the transaction allows.

A decision to acquire, invest in or adopt something is made against a deadline, and usually with less access than an ordinary engagement. We work within both. Before a deal closes we establish the security posture of what is being bought to the depth the available access permits, and separate what is merely untidy from what becomes expensive later. Once it closes, the same engineers examine the acquired systems with the access ownership now allows, and state what has to be resolved before the environments are joined. The same work applies when you are evaluating a product rather than a company: we assess it on your behalf, with no relationship to its vendor. In each case the result is a record on the platform, where every finding carries evidence, severity and a deadline and stays open until it is fixed, so the assessment keeps working for whoever owns the system afterwards.
